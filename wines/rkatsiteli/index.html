The grape

Rkatsiteli is one of Georgia’s oldest grape varieties and its most widely planted white. The name is usually translated as “red stem”. It keeps high natural acidity even in warm years, which is why it carries long skin contact so well.

Fermented on its skins in qvevri, Rkatsiteli turns amber and takes on grip from the tannins in the skins. Amber Rkatsiteli is often described with notes of dried apricot, quince, black tea and walnut.

How Dasabami makes it

At Dasabami, Rkatsiteli comes from vines about 70 years old in Tamarisi, Kvemo Kartli. The grapes ferment with their own ambient yeasts in clay qvevri buried in the earth, including the family’s century-old qvevri, and recent vintages stay on their skins for four to six months.

It is the family’s largest wine, around 800 to 900 bottles a year, bottled unfined, unfiltered and with no added SO₂. Recent vintages carry a painted art label.

Said about it
Questions

Rkatsiteli

What is Dasabami Rkatsiteli?

An amber (skin-contact) natural wine from the Rkatsiteli grape, made by the Darsavelidze family in Tamarisi, Kvemo Kartli, Georgia. It ferments in qvevri with ambient yeasts and is bottled unfined, unfiltered and with no added SO₂.

How long does Dasabami Rkatsiteli stay on its skins?

Recent vintages spend four to six months on their skins in qvevri: six months for the 2020 and four months for the 2022.

How many bottles of Dasabami Rkatsiteli are made?

Around 800 to 900 bottles a year: 900 in 2020 and 800 in 2022.

What food goes with amber Rkatsiteli?

Its grip and acidity suit Georgian food such as walnut dishes, grilled vegetables and khachapuri, and aged cheeses.
